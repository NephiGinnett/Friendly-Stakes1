## PRODUCTION NOTES

- **Setting:** Somewhere casual and lived-in. A couch. A kitchen counter. Somewhere CJ actually spends time — not staged, just real. A phone or laptop visible nearby, ideally showing the Friendly Stakes app.
- **Lighting:** Natural or practical. CJ is the approachable entry point — the person who explains it to you like a friend.
- **Tone:** Genuine excitement without being a pitch. CJ knows something good is happening and wants you to know too. Not a salesperson. A person who's already in and thinks you should be.
- **Takes:** Let him talk. If he finds a better word than what's on the page, keep it. The script is a shape.
- **Costume:** Whatever CJ is wearing. Energy drink can present, not featured.
- **Camera:** Eye level. Conversational. He's talking to the group, not performing for a lens.

## DIRECTOR / STAGING NOTES

**The phone:**
This is the object that grounds the scene. It should be there naturally, not as a prop. If he can actually have Friendly Stakes pulled up on it, great. If not, face-down is fine. The point is that he's someone who has already been in the app before this moment.

**"Five hundred points gets you in."**
Don't rush this. It should feel like a good deal, not a disclaimer. He's inviting, not selling.

**"Five legs — thirteen times."**
The pause before "thirteen times" is the beat. CJ should land on that number like he's still a little impressed by it himself. Not performed surprise — just genuine. If he naturally says "five legs hits and you're getting thirteen times your money back," keep it.

**"Stay in it."**
 — this is encouragement, not observation. CJ is rooting for you. The tagline should feel like something he'd actually say to a friend who was about to close the app.

**Monitor Cans:**
Not mentioned in the script by name. If CJ naturally asks about them or brings them up, that's fine — but the ad doesn't need it. The event features carry the spot. The can is there for branding. Let it be a drink, not a mechanic.

**Pacing:**
Faster than Scott's but not rushed. CJ has energy — use it. The pauses are still doing work, especially after "five legs" and after "MONITOR." Don't let the whole thing blur into one take.

**If CJ naturally says something different:**
Keep it. The three things that must survive: the team pick, the parlay multiplier, and the tagline.
