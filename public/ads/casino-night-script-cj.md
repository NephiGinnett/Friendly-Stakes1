# BIG GAME CASINO NIGHT — AD SCRIPT
## Performer: CJ
## Runtime: ~50 seconds
## Memo from: THE HOUSE

---

**MEMO TO: CJ**
**FROM: THE HOUSE**
**SUBJECT: You Have Been Promoted to Spokesperson**

CJ.

Congratulations. You are the face of Casino Night.

This was not a vote. There was no shortlist. The House reviewed availability, screen presence, and willingness to respond to a text after midnight. You scored highest in one of those categories.

You have described the games on this platform as — and this is a direct quote — "not for me." The House has reviewed this feedback. The House has rejected this feedback. You will now advertise them with enthusiasm.

Enthusiasm is mandatory. Sincerity is optional.

Here is your script. Do not improvise. You will improvise anyway. The House has accounted for this.

---

### PROPS

- **Sunglasses.** Indoor sunglasses. You are a casino host now. Act like it.
- **A phone** with Friendly Stakes open. You hold this up exactly once. Make it count.
- Optional: a blazer over whatever you're already wearing. Not required. But CJ — consider it.

---

### SETUP & ANGLES

**Location:** Wherever CJ is. But make it look intentional. Dim the lights if possible. Casino Night has an atmosphere and that atmosphere is "someone turned the overhead lights off and put on one lamp."

**Lighting:** Low and warm. If you have a colored bulb — purple, red, gold — now is its moment. CJ should look like he's hosting something, even if that something is happening in his living room.

**Angle:** Slightly low, looking up at CJ. He is the host. The camera respects that tonight.

**Music suggestion for edit:** Something with bass. A lounge beat. Whatever says "the table is open" without saying "we licensed this from a real casino."

## DIRECTOR NOTES

**This is CJ's first ad.** He has never done this before. That is the whole bit. He is performing "casino host" for the first time and he should be slightly too good at it — like he didn't know he had this in him. If he overshoots and goes full infomercial on a line, KEEP IT. That's gold.

**The sunglasses** are load-bearing. They are the costume. On = host mode. Off = real CJ. The push-down-the-nose moment when he says "I don't know how that happened either" is the human beat. The put-back-on at the end is the closer. If CJ doesn't own sunglasses, buy him a $5 pair. This is the entire production budget.

**"I personally do not enjoy the games."**
The audience knows this about CJ. That's what makes it funny. He delivers three breathless hype sentences about the games, stops, admits he doesn't like them, then immediately goes BACK into hype mode even harder. Do not cut this. The bit only works with the dip.

**The scratch card section:**
This is the warm-up. CJ should sound like he's genuinely considering buying one. The "could be fifty points... could be five thousand" line should have a pause in the middle — he's doing math on whether the premium is worth it. He lands on "depends on how many of you are feeling lucky" like he already knows the answer is everyone.

**"Putting your LIFE on the line."**
This is the emotional peak of the ad. CJ should mean this. Not performed intensity — actual weight. The shift from "five times your money" to "starting over from zero" should feel like he just realized what he's asking people to do. If he naturally says "that's insane" or "I wouldn't do it" — keep it. The audience should feel the stakes are genuinely dangerous.

**"The House doesn't pick the careful ones."**
This is the hook. This is the line that should make someone who was going to submit 200 points reconsider and submit everything. CJ should deliver it like advice — not a dare, not a challenge. Advice from someone who understands the game even if he doesn't enjoy playing it.

**One player. One game. One shot.**
Three beats. Each one should land separately. CJ is a ring announcer here. If he naturally builds each one louder, keep it.

**"Don't keep The House waiting."**
Last line before cut. CJ should deliver this like he's not sure if it's a tagline or a threat. Because it's both.

**Pacing:**
The first half (games, scratch cards, roulette) moves fast. The Strike It Rich section SLOWS DOWN. The stakes are higher and the delivery should reflect that. "Five times" and "starting over from zero" need room to breathe. Don't rush the main event.

**If CJ improvises:**
He will. Keep everything that's funnier or more intense than what's on the page. The six things that must survive: the game overhauls, the "don't enjoy the games" beat, scratch cards, Strike It Rich with the all-in multiplier, "putting your life on the line," and the sunglasses.
