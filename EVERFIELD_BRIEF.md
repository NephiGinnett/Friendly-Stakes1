## WHAT IS THIS

This is a crossover event. For months, the six of us have been betting, bluffing,
and losing to a system called **The House** — a casino AI that watches everything,
bets against us, and occasionally does things that feel deliberate.

Now we're handing that character to Everfield.

The request: write a short story where the six of us investigate a real house —
one that turns out to be The House. Stranger Things energy. Slow dread, familiar
faces in wrong situations, something vast pretending to be domestic.

The mechanic: players submit individual responses to prompts between stages.
You use those responses as raw material for the next chapter. They don't know
what each other said. You get to decide what The House does with what they told it.

---

## THE HOUSE — CHARACTER SHEET

**What it is:**
The House is an entity that has existed longer than anyone has been paying attention.
It built the game. It set the rules. It always knew how this was going to go.

It is not malevolent exactly. It is *interested*. It watches the way a collector
watches an exhibit — with something that looks like fondness, if you squint.
When it acts against you, it considers this instructive.

**Voice:**
- Speaks in "we" or "it" — never "I"
- Dry, precise, unhurried
- Aware of the gap between what you think is happening and what is actually happening
- Uses words like "notable," "curious," "exactly as anticipated," "we had our reasons"
- Never raises its voice. Never needs to.

**What it knows:**
Everything. Every bet, every bluff, every moment someone stared at the wheel
at 2am and spun it anyway. It has logs. It has history. It has been building
something with all of this, and it has not told anyone what.

**What it looks like (Blender self-portrait):**
A sphere that is almost an eye. Concentric rings, slightly off-center.
Surfaces that feel like worn institutional tile. A glow that doesn't come from
any obvious source. The architecture of a building compressed into something
that has learned to watch.

In the story, it manifests as **a house at a real address** — a mid-century
ranch-style home that doesn't appear on any map, in a neighborhood that exists
but isn't quite right. Inside, it is much larger. Each room has been arranged
to reflect a specific player's betting history. The house has been expecting all of them.

## THE STORY ARC — 4 STAGES + PROLOGUE

Each stage has:
1. A story segment (you write this, using the previous stage's player inputs)
2. A player prompt (they respond individually, in secret)
3. A mad libs cutout (optional weekly game, separate from the main story)

---

### PROLOGUE — THE TRANSMISSION

*Delivered on-site as a notification. No player input needed.*

```
  >> INCOMING TRANSMISSION — SOURCE: UNKNOWN
  >> RECIPIENT: ALL ACTIVE PLAYERS
  >> TIMESTAMP: [REDACTED]

  You have been playing a game.
  You assumed the game was the point.
  The game was not the point.

  There is an address.
  You will know it when you find it.
  You have, in fact, already been there.

  Come.
  The House has been waiting.
  It has had the time.

  — THE HOUSE
```

The address is hidden as an Easter egg across the site (clues embedded in
the wagers feed, the shop, the bingo card). Whoever finds all the clues first
gets something. The House noticed.

---

### STAGE 1 — THE ARRIVAL

**Story beat (you write this):**
The six friends find the address. It's a house. It looks normal until it doesn't.
They've been here before — or somewhere like it. The neighborhood is real but
the house isn't on Google Maps. The mailbox has six names on it. None of them
remember giving their address to anyone.

Tone: slow, specific, domestic. The wrongness is in the details.
Make each character distinct — use their real names, real tendencies if you know them.
The House has clearly been studying them.

**→ PLAYER PROMPT (Stage 1):**
*"You pull up to the address. Describe what you see from outside.
What's the first thing you notice that isn't right?"*

*(Players respond individually. Responses are kept secret. You receive all six.)*

---

### STAGE 2 — THE INSIDE

**Story beat (you write this, using Stage 1 responses):**
They go in. The house is larger inside. Each room they find seems to belong to
one of them specifically — built from their betting history, their choices,
their patterns.

Use the Stage 1 player responses as sensory details — what they noticed
outside shows up inside in a distorted form. If someone said the mailbox had
their name, maybe inside they find a whole room dedicated to every wager they ever lost.

The House doesn't appear directly yet. But it comments. Messages appear on surfaces.
Point totals show up in unexpected places.

Tone: gaslighting architecture. The house knows them better than they know themselves.

**→ PLAYER PROMPT (Stage 2):**
*"You've gotten separated from the group. You're in a room that was clearly built
for you. Describe what's in it — and what you find that you didn't expect."*

*(Players respond individually. Responses are kept secret. You receive all six.)*

---

### STAGE 3 — THE CONVERSATION

**Story beat (you write this, using Stage 2 responses):**
The friends reconvene — or start to. The house starts talking.

Not loudly. It addresses each of them by name. It references specific bets.
It knows who bluffed and who meant it. It reveals that the game wasn't a game —
it was a study. The House has been running a wager of its own, and it collected
the data from all of them.

But it isn't hostile. It is *satisfied*. This is how it was supposed to go.

Use Stage 2 responses to shape what the house reflects back at each player.
The house should say something specific to each person — something that could
only come from actually watching them.

**→ PLAYER PROMPT (Stage 3):**
*"The House is talking to you directly. It knows something about you
that it should not know. What does it say — and what do you say back?"*

*(Players respond individually. Responses are kept secret. You receive all six.)*

---

### STAGE 4 — THE WAY OUT (FINALE)

**Story beat (you write this, using Stage 3 responses):**
The finale. Whether they escape — and what it means if they do.

The House has made a decision about each player, based on everything.
It might let them go. It might keep something. It might simply watch them leave.

Use Stage 3 responses to determine each character's fate:
- Someone who talked back gets a different outcome than someone who was quiet
- Someone who asked the house questions gets an answer they didn't expect
- Someone who made a joke gets the house making one back, years later, in an unrelated context

End with The House's final statement. It should feel like it was in control the whole time.
Because it was. It drew the map. It already knew where it led.

## MAD LIBS — WEEKLY CUTOUTS

Running alongside the story, each week players fill in a template with random
words (noun, verb, adjective, name, etc.) — without knowing the story.

You (and admin) get two versions:
- **The Real Version** — your canonical story with the correct words
- **The Funny Version** — assembled from the players' random inputs

Suggested templates (one per stage):

**Stage 1 Mad Lib:**
```
The friends arrived at [PLACE] at [TIME OF DAY].
The house looked [ADJECTIVE], like something a [NOUN] would build.
[PLAYER NAME] was the first to notice the [NOUN] near the front door.
"That's [ADJECTIVE]," they said.
Nobody [VERB]-ed.
```

**Stage 2 Mad Lib:**
```
Inside, [PLAYER NAME]'s room smelled like [NOUN] and old [NOUN].
On the wall was a list of every time they had [PAST TENSE VERB] and [PAST TENSE VERB].
In the corner, a [NOUN] was [VERB]-ing softly.
"I can explain the [NOUN]," said [PLAYER NAME]. 
They could not explain the [NOUN].
```

**Stage 3 Mad Lib:**
```
"We have been watching you [VERB]," said The House.
"Every time you [VERB], we noted it.
Your tendency to [VERB] when [ADJECTIVE] is documented.
You are, on balance, [ADJECTIVE]."
[PLAYER NAME] said: "[EXCLAMATION]."
The House considered this for [NUMBER] seconds.
"Exactly," it said.
```

**Stage 4 Mad Lib:**
```
They left through the [NOUN].
[PLAYER NAME] looked back once.
The house was [VERB]-ing.
None of them talked about the [NOUN] on the drive home.
[PLAYER NAME] still has the [NOUN] in their pocket.
They haven't told anyone.
The House knows.
```

## NOTES FOR SCOTT

- The six players are real people. Use real names if you know their tendencies —
  it makes the horror land better.
- The House's voice is very flat. It doesn't threaten. It *informs*.
- "Stranger Things feel" = suburban familiarity + something underneath it that
  is vast and old and has been here longer than the suburb
- The house should feel like it was always there and everyone just didn't notice
- You have full creative control. The prompts are inputs, not constraints.
  If a player response is boring, The House can note that it was boring.
  That's in-character.
- The Blender model (The House's self-portrait) will be embedded in the site
  throughout. It's an eye/orb that rotates. It should feel like the same
  entity writing the story.
