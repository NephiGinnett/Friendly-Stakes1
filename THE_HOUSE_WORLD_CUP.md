## § 0 — A NOTE FROM THE HOUSE

Every four years, the world stops to watch men kick a ball into a net. The House has observed this phenomenon many times. It finds it efficient — so much happening, so many opinions formed, so many of those opinions expressed with more confidence than the evidence warrants.

This creates an ideal environment for the things we do here.

The World Cup Event is not a replacement for the rest of the platform. It is an addition. The wagers continue. The wheel spins. The bingo card sits there, waiting. Everything you already know still applies. This is simply another room in the building — and the building has been here the whole time.

Read carefully. The event has more moving parts than usual, and The House has found that players who understand the mechanics fare significantly better than those who guess. It is not invested in your success, exactly. It simply prefers an informed opponent.

Participating in the World Cup Event costs **500 points**. This is not a stake. It does not come back if your team loses. It goes somewhere specific, and Section II will explain exactly where.

In exchange, you choose a team. One team. The one you believe in — or the one you are willing to pretend to believe in for the duration of the tournament. The House does not require sincerity. It requires commitment.

```

  ║   1 team   →  your allegiance                ║

  ║   The House noted that you read this part.   ║
  ║                                              ║
  ╚══════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

Once the bracket locks — which happens the moment the first match of the Round of 32 kicks off — picks and entries are sealed. There is no editing. There is no late registration. The window is exactly as wide as it looks, and then it closes.

The House recommends not waiting until the last minute. It has seen what happens to players who do. It was not particularly surprised.

  │  THE ALLEGIANCE POOL                                         │

```

Every entry fee — 500 points from each player, plus any proxy fees paid during the event — accumulates into a single shared pool. The House holds it. It does not touch it. It simply watches it grow.

When the tournament ends and a champion is crowned, every player who backed the winning team splits the pool equally.

```
  All entry fees + all proxy fees
  ──────────────────────────────  =  each winner's share
       Number of winners
```

This means three things worth understanding:

**First** — the fewer players who backed the champion, the larger each winner's share. Backing a favourite is comfortable. Backing a longshot pays better. The House does not tell you what to do with this information. It simply provides it.

**Second** — original allegiance takes priority. If any players picked the winning team as their **first choice** at entry, the entire pool goes to them and proxy backers receive nothing. Proxies only split the pot if every single backer of the winning team arrived there via proxy — meaning no one called it from the start. The House finds this outcome unlikely. It has accounted for it anyway.

**Third** — proxy fees count regardless of who wins. Every time a player pays to back a new team after elimination, that 250 points enters the pool. Even if that player never wins a penny, their money is already in there. There will be more on that in Section VI.

```

The Confidence Wager is the event's primary financial instrument. It works as follows:

You and another player each stake a number of points. You are backing your team. They are backing theirs. The teams play each other. The winner of the match collects both stakes.

Simple in concept. The House has added a wrinkle.

```
  ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░
  ░                                                ░
  ░   N E P H I ' S   R U L E                          ░
  ░                                                ░
  ░   When the lower stake is less than half       ░
  ░   of the higher stake, the wager becomes       ░
  ░   asymmetric.                                  ░
  ░                                                ░
  ░   HIGH-STAKE player wins:                      ░
  ░     → receives both stakes                     ░
  ░     → plus a 33% bonus on the high stake       ░
  ░       (paid by the system, not the loser)      ░
  ░                                                ░
  ░   LOW-STAKE player wins:                       ░
  ░     → receives both stakes                     ░
  ░     → minus 33% of the high stake              ░
  ░       (disappears from the pot entirely)       ░
  ░                                                ░
  ░   The House finds this rule elegant.           ░
  ░   Nephi designed it. The House respects it.        ░
  ░                                                ░
  ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░
```

The logic: a large stake deserves protection against a small one. Committing 300 points against someone's 50 should not carry the same risk profile as a symmetric bet. Nephi's Rule corrects for that. Whether it corrects in your favour depends on which side you are on.

**Draws** — if the match ends level, both players receive their stakes back. No record is created. The House considers this the least interesting outcome, but allows it.

**The Fan Competition cut** — 10% of the winner's net profit from every settled confidence wager is redirected to a separate pot. Section IX covers what happens to it.

**One restriction:** Confidence wagers are only available to players whose original team is still in the tournament. If your team has been eliminated, this table is closed to you. The proxy option — Section VI — reopens parlays, but not this.

A parlay is a multi-leg wager. One player proposes it. Another player accepts each leg individually.

Each leg is a single prediction about a single match outcome. You choose a team. You choose a stake per leg. The available stake tiers are:

```
  100 pts per leg  →  1 🥤 per accepted leg
  200 pts per leg  →  2 🥤 per accepted leg
  300 pts per leg  →  3 🥤 per accepted leg
```

The Monitor Cans shown above are earned by the **proposer** simply when a leg is accepted — regardless of whether the prediction is correct. Getting someone to take the other side is itself a reward. The House finds this detail interesting to watch play out.

If a leg is correct, additional cans are awarded at settlement. Both sides earn them.

A parlay can contain up to 5 legs. Getting 3 or more correct unlocks an achievement. Getting all 5 correct unlocks a different, significantly more rewarding one. Neither is guaranteed. The House is aware of this. It noted it anyway.

Teams get eliminated. This is the nature of knockout football, and The House has no particular sympathy for it — though it does find the resulting scramble among players quite instructive.

When your team is knocked out, you have a choice. You may:

**Do nothing** — remain in the event. Your bracket picks still count if those predictions come in. You have nothing further to stake and nothing left to win in the allegiance pool. You watch. Some players prefer this. The House finds their company acceptable.

**Pick a proxy team** — pay **250 points** to redirect your allegiance to a new team still alive in the tournament.

```
  ╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
  ║                                                   ║
  ║   PROXY RULES                                     ║
  ║                                                   ║
  ║   · Cost: 250 pts every time you pick             ║
  ║   · Each pick goes into the allegiance pool       ║
  ║   · You may switch as many times as you like      ║
  ║     (and can afford)                              ║
  ║   · Parlays are available with your proxy team    ║
  ║   · Confidence wagers remain closed               ║
  ║   · Your confidence stake is locked at zero       ║
  ║   · If your proxy team is also eliminated,        ║
  ║     you may pick again — for another 250 pts      ║
  ║                                                   ║
  ╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

There is no free change. Each selection costs 250 points. The fee goes directly into the allegiance pool, which means it benefits whoever eventually wins. You are, in a sense, contributing to someone else's windfall with every switch. The House finds this arrangement beautifully structured. It did not design it. It simply benefits from observing it.

A proxy player who backed the eventual champion at the time of final settlement **shares in the pool equally with original backers**. The path to the pick does not matter. What matters is who you backed when the final whistle blew.

Every confidence wager that settles produces a winner. That winner earns a net profit. Ten percent of that net profit does not go to the winner — it goes into a separate pot called the Fan Competition fund.

This is not optional. It is automatic. The House deducts it before the payout reaches you.

Over the course of the tournament, this pot grows with every wager settled. The standings page tracks each player's cumulative net profit from confidence wagers — this is their **Fan Score**. At the end of the event, the two players with the highest Fan Scores split the pot.

```
  ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  1st place  →  65% of total pot                        │
  │  2nd place  →  remaining 35%                           │
  │                                                        │
  │  Fan Score = total net profit from confidence wagers   │
  │             (win more, score higher)                   │
  └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The House finds the Fan Competition elegant. It rewards the same players who already won on confidence wagers — and it does so with money those same players contributed. The pot builds because people played. The prize goes to whoever played best. Nothing is wasted. The House approves of systems that contain no waste.
